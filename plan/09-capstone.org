#+TITLE: Capstone: StarFest conditions monitor

A small device for astronomy outreach nights: it measures temperature,
humidity, and pressure, warns when dew is likely, logs everything, and sends
readings to mrshudson so anyone on the home network can see recent
conditions. It grows across three versions, each built inside the regular
weeks. This page describes the goal; progress is tracked in those weeks.

* Version 1: standalone logger (week 24)

- Reads temperature, humidity, and pressure from a BME280 at a chosen interval.
- Shows the current readings on an SSD1306 OLED.
- Appends timestamped readings to a CSV file on a microSD card.
- Handles a disconnected or misbehaving sensor: shows an error, keeps
  running, and recovers when the sensor returns.
- Rejects impossible values and counts errors.

Built on a breadboard with MicroPython, from weeks 20–23.

* Version 2: networked (week 25, moved to protoboard in week 28)

- The Pico 2 W sends each reading over Wi-Fi to a small HTTP service on
  mrshudson that you write.
- mrshudson stores readings (SQLite on the bakerstreet drive is a good fit)
  and serves a page with the last 24 hours.
- Dew point is calculated with the Magnus formula, and the page shows a
  warning when the temperature is within 2–3 °C of the dew point.
- While the server or Wi-Fi is down, the Pico buffers readings on the SD card
  and sends them later.
- The mrshudson service runs under systemd and survives reboots.

Each device has one job: the Pico measures, mrshudson stores and presents.

* Version 3: your own PCB (weeks 29–32)

- The same circuit on a two-layer board you designed in KiCad, with the Pico
  and modules in sockets.
- The sensor sits away from the Pico and regulator to keep its readings true.
- Brought up in stages and run for 24 hours.

* What it can and can't tell you

Temperature, humidity, pressure, and dew point tell you when optics will fog
and whether conditions are changing. They don't tell you whether the sky is
clear, how transparent it is, or how steady the seeing will be. Those depend
on clouds and on the atmosphere well above the ground.

* Ideas for later

- A battery and an enclosure, using the week 26 current budget.
- A sky-quality sensor or an infrared thermometer pointed up (a cold sky
  reading suggests clear skies).
- Several monitors around a site, all reporting to the same mrshudson page.
